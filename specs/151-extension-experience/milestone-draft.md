# Local milestone draft — NetClaw's editor workbench

Publication is explicitly held. This is a local draft for the owner, not a published post.

Spec151 turns the VS Code extension into a more coherent network workbench: theme-aware cards, clear form labels, preserved blue actions, local branded artwork and a BGP-session view with purposeful 3D and a readable fallback. Connection setup now explains what belongs in each input.

The visual work also made two operator workflows concrete. Testbed management supports guided devices, advanced YAML and reviewed YAML/JSON/CSV imports with conflict detection, credential protection and private recovery. MCP cards bring configuration fields, setup guidance and explicitly reviewed supported operations into context.

A subsequent owner amendment brings DefenseClaw tool rules and enforcement defaults, plus OpenShell sandbox network policies, into the same reviewable workbench. Status and redacted logs remain distinct from saved configuration and actual enforcement. Explicit shared-target binding and acknowledgment of weaker protection keep the controls understandable. Local contract fixtures verify behavior; live vendor enforcement remains unqualified.

The key engineering lesson was to preserve ownership where it already exists. The HUD owns testbed sessions and collector consent; the existing installer/runtime helpers own their lifecycle work. The extension presents those responsibilities clearly and verifies outcomes without inventing service health or silently replaying interrupted work.

Local evidence and platform limits are recorded in verification.md. The work remains a source-development candidate until the owner explicitly requests packaging or distribution.
